\title{Photon condensation and macroscopic quantum tunnelling of the persistent current in a $\pi$ loop}

\author{\ldots}
\affiliation{\ldots}

\begin{abstract}
A superconducting loop closed by a $\pi$ junction carries a spontaneous persistent current when its
inductance exceeds the Josephson inductance of the junction. We ask what happens when that inductance
belongs to a quantum LC mode and the $\pi$ junction is an interacting quantum dot, so that the loop is a
circuit-QED element and the flux a quantum variable. Solving the full electron--photon problem by DMRG,
we show that the current appears as a static displacement of the mode, i.e.\ a photon condensate, when
the dot is in its doublet ($\pi$) state and the screening parameter $\beta=2\pi LI_c/\Phi_0$ exceeds
one. The criterion is the single-loop form of the photon-condensation instability, which the flux
coupling is allowed to reach because the $\pi$ shift makes the orbital response paramagnetic. Quantum
fluctuations of the flux do not drive the transition but oppose it: the exact ground state is a cat of
the two current directions, split by a tunnelling gap $\delta\propto e^{-E_J/\hbar\omega}$. We map the
phase diagram in the interaction and coupling strengths, show where the self-consistent product-state
(mean-field) description holds and where it fails, and give the scales for quantum-dot, kinetic-inductance
and SFS realizations.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\paragraph*{Introduction.}
A superconducting ring interrupted by a $\pi$ junction has a ground state that carries a current without
any applied magnetic field~\cite{BKS1977}. The junction prefers a Cooper-pair phase difference of $\pi$,
the loop can only supply it through a flux $\Phi_0/2$, and when the loop inductance $L$ is large enough that
the magnetic cost is below the Josephson gain, $\beta\equiv2\pi LI_c/\Phi_0>1$, the ring settles into one
of two degenerate states carrying opposite currents $\pm I$. Such half-flux states are textbook evidence of $d$-wave pairing~\cite{Tsuei2000} and of the $\pi$ shift
in superconductor--ferromagnet--superconductor (SFS) junctions~\cite{Ryazanov2001,Bauer2004}; in all of
them the flux is a classical variable, because $E_J$ exceeds every other energy scale by orders of
magnitude.

Two developments make it natural to revisit this problem as a quantum one. First, $\pi$ junctions can now
be made from gate-tunable quantum dots, where the $\pi$ shift is produced by a single unpaired spin
and the Josephson energy is a few GHz~\cite{GlazmanMatveev1989,SpivakKivelson1991,vanDam2006,Cleuziou2006,Delagrange2015}.
Second, the loop inductance can be the inductance of a superconducting LC resonator, so that the loop is a
circuit-QED element~\cite{Blais2021} whose flux is the quadrature of a photon mode with $\hbar\omega\sim E_J$.
The ring current then becomes a static displacement of the mode. Whether a cavity field can condense in the ground state of a light--matter system is a debated question:
a no-go theorem forbids it for a uniform field coupled to the charge~\cite{Andolina2019,Nataf2019}, while
a field coupling to the orbital magnetization can condense if the matter has a paramagnetic orbital
response~\cite{Andolina2020}. A $\pi$ loop is the simplest system in which that response is paramagnetic by
construction.

In this Letter we solve the problem without approximation. We treat a lattice model of a superconducting
loop closed by an Anderson impurity and threaded by the flux of a quantum LC mode by DMRG on the full
electron--photon Hilbert space, and compare with the adiabatic (Born--Oppenheimer) reduction and with the
self-consistent product-state mean field that is customarily used for such problems. We find (i) a phase
diagram in the plane of the dot interaction $U$ and the coupling $g$ whose two boundaries are the $0$--$\pi$
transition of the dot and the classical screening criterion $\beta=1$; (ii) a photon condensate of
$\langle a^\dagger a\rangle\simeq8$ carrying a current of $0.95\,I_c$, whose direction is chosen
spontaneously because the Hamiltonian is time-reversal symmetric; (iii) a ground state that is, strictly, a
cat of the two current directions, whose tunnel splitting $\delta$ vanishes as $e^{-E_J/\hbar\omega}$ and
sets the only energy scale between them; and (iv) the condition $E_J\gg\hbar\omega$ under which the product-state mean field is reliable, with
explicit examples where it predicts a current that the exact solution does not have.

\paragraph*{Model.}
We consider the circuit of Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(a): a superconducting (SC) chain of $L-1$ sites closed
into a loop by a quantum dot (site 0), with the loop threaded by the flux of a single lumped LC mode,
\begin{align}
  H &= \hbar\omega\, a^\dagger a
     -t\!\sum_{r=1}^{L-2}\sum_\sigma\!\left(c^\dagger_{r+1\sigma}c_{r\sigma}+{\rm h.c.}\right)
     \nonumber\\
    &\quad +\Delta\!\sum_{r=1}^{L-1}\!\left(c^\dagger_{r\uparrow}c^\dagger_{r\downarrow}+{\rm h.c.}\right)
     +\epsilon_d\, n_0 + U n_{0\uparrow}n_{0\downarrow}
     \nonumber\\
    &\quad -t_J\sum_\sigma\left(c^\dagger_{0\sigma}c_{L-1\,\sigma}+{\rm h.c.}\right)
     \nonumber\\
    &\quad -t_J\sum_\sigma\left(e^{i\varphi_{\rm cl}}\,e^{ig(a+a^\dagger)}\,c^\dagger_{1\sigma}c_{0\sigma}+{\rm h.c.}\right).
  \label{eq:H}
\end{align}
The first two lines are the mode and a BCS chain with hopping $t$ and a fixed pair field $\Delta$, plus the
Anderson impurity (level $\epsilon_d=-U/2$, on-site repulsion $U$) coupled by $t_J$ to the two ends of
the chain. The last line carries the total Peierls phase of the loop. Three remarks fix its meaning.
(i) The phase is a loop property and can be placed on a single bond by a gauge choice; Eq.~\eqref{eq:H} is
identical to a uniform vector potential along the ring with the pair field transformed covariantly,
$\Delta_r\to\Delta e^{-2i\theta_r}$~\cite{Li2020,Dmytruk2021}, whereas a uniform $A$ with a real $\Delta$
is a different model~\cite{SM}.
(ii) The mode enters through the displacement operator $e^{ig(a+a^\dagger)}$, so diamagnetic ($A^2$) and all
higher terms are included. (iii) $g=2\pi\Phi_{\rm zpf}/(h/e)$ is the \emph{electron} phase produced by the
zero-point flux of the mode; one SC flux quantum $\Phi_0=h/2e$ corresponds to an electron phase $\pi$. For a
lumped resonator of impedance $Z$ in series with the loop, $(2g)^2=\pi Z/R_Q$, $R_Q=h/4e^2$.
A fixed, real $\Delta$ means that the condensate phase of the arms is pinned (a proximitized ring);
the kinetic inductance of a self-supporting ring then simply adds to $L$, see below.
Unless stated otherwise $t=1$, $\Delta=t$, $t_J=0.5t$, $L=14$, $\hbar\omega=7.6\times10^{-4}t$ and
there is \emph{no} static flux: $\varphi_{\rm cl}\to0^+$, with a seed $\varphi_{\rm cl}=10^{-3}$ (or
$10^{-5}$) used only to select one of two degenerate states. We write Eq.~\eqref{eq:H} compactly as
$H=\hbar\omega a^\dagger a+H_{\rm ring}[\varphi_{\rm cl}+g(a+a^\dagger)]$.

The only property of the ring that will matter is its ground-state energy at fixed phase,
\begin{equation}
  E(\varphi)=\sum_{m\ge0}E_m\cos m\varphi ,
  \label{eq:harm}
\end{equation}
where even harmonics are Cooper-pair ($h/2e$) processes and $E_1$ is the single-electron ($h/e$)
persistent current of a mesoscopic ring~\cite{LossGoldbart1991}, suppressed as $e^{-L/\xi}$. A
$0$-junction has $E_2<0$, a $\pi$-junction $E_2>0$. Since $H_{\rm ring}[0]$ is real, $E(\varphi)=E(-\varphi)$
exactly: the two current directions are related by time reversal. This is what distinguishes an intrinsic
$\pi$ junction from a $0$-junction biased by $\Phi_0/2$: the latter maps $E_2\to-E_2$ but turns the odd
harmonics into an explicit tilt $-E_1\sin\varphi$ that \emph{selects} the current direction (Supplemental
Material, SM~\cite{SM}).

\paragraph*{Adiabatic reduction and the classical criterion.}
When $\hbar\omega$ is well below the Andreev gap $E_A$ of the ring, the electrons follow the flux
adiabatically and Eq.~\eqref{eq:H} reduces, to leading order in $\hbar\omega/E_A$, to a single-particle
problem for the dimensionless flux $X=a+a^\dagger$,
\begin{equation}
  H_{\rm BO}=\frac{\hbar\omega}{4}\left(P^2+X^2\right)+E(\varphi_{\rm cl}+gX),\qquad [X,P]=2i .
  \label{eq:BO}
\end{equation}
This is the Hamiltonian of an rf-SQUID~\cite{Friedman2000} whose inductive energy is supplied by the mode:
in the Cooper-pair phase $\theta=2gX$ it reads $4E_Cn^2+\tfrac12E_L\theta^2+E(\theta/2)$ with
$E_L=\hbar\omega/8g^2$ and $E_C=\hbar\omega g^2$, the fluxonium form~\cite{Manucharyan2009}. The classical
energy $\mathcal F(X)=\hbar\omega X^2/4+E(gX)$ develops two minima $\pm X^\star$ when the orbital response
of the ring at $\varphi=0$ is paramagnetic and exceeds the stiffness of the mode,
\begin{equation}
  \beta\equiv\frac{2g^2|E''(0)|}{\hbar\omega}>1,\qquad E''(0)<0 .
  \label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
For a pure $\pi$ junction $E''(0)=-4E_J$, and $\beta=8g^2E_J/\hbar\omega=2\pi LI_c/\Phi_0$ with
$I_c=2eE_J/\hbar$ is the screening parameter of Ref.~\cite{BKS1977}. Equation~\eqref{eq:beta} is the
single-mode form of the photon-condensation criterion of Ref.~\cite{Andolina2020}: a static field
$\langle a\rangle\neq0$ appears because the mode couples to the flux (the curl of $\mathbf A$), to which the
no-go theorem for a uniform field does not apply, and because the $\pi$ shift makes the response
paramagnetic. A $0$-junction, with $E''(0)>0$, never orders.

The static field is a persistent current. Indeed $\langle[H,P]\rangle=0$ in any eigenstate gives the exact
identity
\begin{equation}
  \langle I\rangle=\frac{\hbar\omega}{2g}\,\langle a+a^\dagger\rangle,\qquad I\equiv-\frac{e}{\hbar}\,\partial_\varphi H_{\rm ring},
  \label{eq:virial}
\end{equation}
which says that the current around the loop \emph{is} the displacement of the mode, and a photon number
$\langle a^\dagger a\rangle\simeq(X^\star/2)^2$ is the condensate. We use Eq.~\eqref{eq:virial} to extract
the current from DMRG, where the one-site observable $\langle X\rangle$ converges far better than the
three-site bond current.

Two remarks complete the classical picture. First, Eq.~\eqref{eq:beta} contains the total loop
inductance. If the mode is coupled to the loop by a mutual inductance $M$ rather than galvanically, minimizing the static
energy over the resonator flux gives a loop energy $\Phi_r^2/2L_r$ with $L_r\ge M^2/L_c$, so a resonator
cannot lower the static threshold; Eq.~\eqref{eq:H} is the galvanic limit. Likewise, if the arms of the
ring are a self-supporting superconductor their kinetic inductance $L_K$ adds to $L$: for our 14-site
chain with $\Delta$ determined self-consistently, we find $\beta_K=2\pi L_KI_c/\Phi_0\simeq0.4$ at $U=2t$~\cite{SM},
so the mode has to supply only the remainder. Second, and central to what follows, the zero-point
fluctuations of the mode do \emph{not} drive the transition; they oppose it. At finite $E_J/\hbar\omega$ the
two minima are connected by tunnelling, the ground state is the symmetric cat
$(|\!\cw\rangle+|\!\ccw\rangle)/\sqrt2$, $\langle I\rangle=0$, and the two current states are split by
$\delta\sim\hbar\omega\,e^{-S}$ with an instanton action $S\propto(E_J/\hbar\omega)\,s(\beta)$. A sharp
transition with mean-field exponents is recovered only in the limit $E_J/\hbar\omega\to\infty$ at fixed
$\beta$, where $E_C/E_J=\beta(\hbar\omega/E_J)^2/8\to0$; this is the classical-oscillator limit of the quantum
Rabi model~\cite{Hwang2015}. The phase diagram of this crossover is Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(b).

\paragraph*{Methods.}
We solve Eq.~\eqref{eq:H} by DMRG (TeNPy~\cite{TeNPy}) on the full electron--photon space: the ring is
stored in folded order next to a bosonic site (Fock cutoff $N_{\rm ph}=30$--$50$), fermion parity and $S^z$
are conserved, and the vertex $e^{ig(a+a^\dagger)}c^\dagger_{1\sigma}c_{0\sigma}$ is a three-site term.
DMRG reproduces exact BdG energies at $g=0$ to $10^{-14}$ and, for a 4-site ring with a photon, exact
diagonalization and Eq.~\eqref{eq:virial} to $10^{-7}$. In production ($\chi=256$--$400$) ground-state
energies are converged to $3\times10^{-6}t$ and excited states (DMRG orthogonal to the ground state) to
$3\times10^{-5}t\approx0.04\hbar\omega$. The $(U,g)$ phase diagram is
obtained in two steps that are each exact: DMRG of the ring at fixed phase in both parity sectors gives
$E(\varphi;U)$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:harm}, on a grid of $U$ (16 values, 9--17 phases each, interpolated in
between); then the photon is treated exactly (full Fock space, no product ansatz) in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:BO}. The only approximation is adiabaticity, $\hbar\omega/E_A\sim10^{-3}$, which we check
against full DMRG at $U=2t$ and $U=6t$ below. The mean-field (MF) reference is the self-consistent product
state $|\psi_{\rm el}\rangle\otimes|\psi_{\rm ph}\rangle$~\cite{notebook}, with Hartree--Fock BdG electrons and
the exact photon state in the field of $\langle c^\dagger_1c_0\rangle$, swept upward in $g$ with a warm start.

\paragraph*{The junction: interaction-driven $\pi$ shift.}
Figure~\ref{fig:junctions} characterizes the ring without the mode. DMRG in the even and odd
fermion-parity sectors reproduces the known singlet--doublet transition of a quantum dot between
superconductors~\cite{GlazmanMatveev1989,SpivakKivelson1991,Vecino2003,Karrasch2008}: for $U<U_c\simeq0.9t$
the singlet is the ground state at $\varphi=0$ and $E_2<0$ (a $0$-junction), for $U>U_c$ the Kramers
doublet is the ground state at all phases and $E(\varphi)$ has its minimum at the Cooper-pair phase $\pi$.
At $U=2t$ we find $E_2=+7.39\times10^{-3}t$ with $|E_1|,|E_4|<10^{-4}t$: a pure $\pi$ junction with
$E_J=|E''(0)|/4=7.6\times10^{-3}t$, i.e.\ $E_J/\hbar\omega=10$ for our mode, and an excitation gap $E_A\simeq0.26t\gg\hbar\omega$. The $\pi$ coupling decreases monotonically with $U$
($E_2\propto U^{-1.3}$ for $4t\le U\le10t$), as expected for cotunnelling through a singly occupied level;
at fixed $\hbar\omega$ the axis $U$ is therefore also an axis of decreasing $E_J/\hbar\omega$, from 15 at
$U=t$ to 1.5 at $U=10t$. The ring is longer than the coherence length ($L\gg\xi\simeq2t/\Delta$), so the
mesoscopic $h/e$ harmonic is negligible; this is essential, because in a short ring $E_1$ dominates
$E(\varphi)$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:junctions}(a)] and the instability would be that of the normal persistent
current rather than of the Josephson junction~\cite{SM}.

\paragraph*{Phase diagram.}
Figure~\ref{fig:main} is the central result. Panel (a) follows the ground state at $U=2t$ as the coupling
grows. For $g<g_c\simeq0.12$ the mode stays near its vacuum and the current is the linear response to the
seed, $|\langle I\rangle|\lesssim10^{-4}\,et/\hbar$, scaling with it. At $g_c$ the photon number jumps to
$\langle a^\dagger a\rangle\simeq8$ and a current $|\langle I\rangle|=1.4\times10^{-2}\,et/\hbar$, i.e.\ $0.95\,I_c$,
appears; reducing the seed by two orders of magnitude leaves it unchanged. The current is spontaneous.
Full DMRG of the electron--photon problem (circles) and the exact photon in Eq.~\eqref{eq:BO} (lines)
agree to a few percent in $g_c$, current and photon number, so adiabaticity holds and the two-step
procedure is justified. Beyond its maximum the current decreases as $I\simeq\hbar\omega\varphi^\star/2g^2$:
the condensate flux saturates at $\Phi_0/2$ (where the junction current vanishes) while the effective
inductance $\propto g^2/\hbar\omega$ keeps growing. The classical threshold is $\beta=1$ at $g=0.112$;
quantum fluctuations shift the observed onset to $0.12$--$0.13$ and round it.

Panel (b) shows $|\langle I\rangle|$ in the $(U,g)$ plane. The ordered region has two boundaries. The lower
one, $U_c\simeq0.9t$, is the $0$--$\pi$ transition of the junction itself: below it the ring is a singlet
with $E''(0)>0$, the orbital response is diamagnetic and no current appears at any $g$ [the boundary bends
down slightly at large $g$, where the condensation energy of the doublet sector tips the parity balance].
The left boundary, $g_c(U)$, follows the classical line $\beta(U,g)=1$ (white dashed), shifted to larger $g$
by the zero-point energy, and moves to stronger coupling as $E_J(U)$ decreases. At $U=t$, $2t$, $4t$ we find
$g_c=0.10$, $0.13$, $0.19$ against the classical $0.092$, $0.112$, $0.155$.

The upper boundary of the coloured region is \emph{not} a phase boundary but a crossover controlled by the
tunnel splitting, panel (c). Along the $U$ axis $E_J/\hbar\omega$ decreases, and $\delta$, which is
$\sim10^{-8}\hbar\omega$ at $U=2t$ deep in the ordered phase, grows to $10^{-4}\hbar\omega$ at $U=4t$,
$10^{-2}\hbar\omega$ at $U=6t$ and $10^{-1}\hbar\omega$ at $U=8t$. A seed flux $\varphi_{\rm cl}$ polarizes
the cat only if $I^\star\varphi_{\rm cl}\gtrsim\delta$; this is why the two contours in (b) separate for
$U\gtrsim4t$ and why the current of the $10^{-3}$ seed, $0.95\,I_c$ at $U=2t$, decays to $0.5\,I_c$ at
$U=6t$ and $0.15\,I_c$ at $U=7t$. The condensate itself is still there: at $U=6t$ the lowest doublet carries
a flux $\Phi^\star=0.6\,\Phi_0/2$ and $\langle a^\dagger a\rangle=2.4$ photons, but the two directions are
coherently mixed. Any experiment resolves this crossover with its own scale: a flux offset $\delta\Phi$ or a
temperature $T$ selects a current direction when $I^\star\delta\Phi$ or $k_BT$ exceeds $\delta$.

\paragraph*{Cat state and macroscopic quantum tunnelling.}
Figure~\ref{fig:udot} resolves the ordered phase without any seed. Since $H$ is real, $\langle I\rangle=0$ in
every eigenstate and the order is diagnosed by the lowest doublet: the splitting $\delta=E_1-E_0$ collapses,
the flux projected onto the doublet acquires eigenvalues $\pm\Phi^\star$, and the flux distribution of the
DMRG ground state, extracted from the photon reduced density matrix, turns from a Gaussian into two peaks at
$\pm\Phi^\star$ whose separation follows the classical minimum [panel (c)]. At $E_J/\hbar\omega=10$ DMRG and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:BO} agree on $\Phi^\star$ to $3\%$ for all $\beta$ and on $\delta/\hbar\omega=0.71$ and $0.28$
at $\beta=0.5$ and $1$; deeper in the ordered phase $\delta$ falls below the DMRG resolution, and the
adiabatic theory gives $7\times10^{-4}$, $2\times10^{-6}$ and $2\times10^{-8}$ at $\beta=1.5$, $2$ and $3$.
At $U=6t$ ($E_J/\hbar\omega=3.2$) the splitting stays resolvable and full DMRG again agrees with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:BO} [squares]. With increasing $E_J/\hbar\omega$ the crossover sharpens into a pitchfork at
$\beta=1$ and $\delta$ vanishes exponentially [panel (b)]. The residual $\delta$ is the macroscopic quantum
tunnelling of a persistent current of $\sim10$ photons between two time-reversed states; because the two
states are related by time reversal, no other energy scale separates them. Each current state is also
Kramers degenerate in the spin of the dot, so the low-energy manifold is the fourfold multiplet
$\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}\otimes\{\cw,\ccw\}$ split only by $\delta$.

\paragraph*{When does mean field work?}
Panel (d) of Fig.~\ref{fig:main} shows the same map in the product-state mean field. It reproduces $U_c$
and the condensate boundary $\langle a^\dagger a\rangle=1$ to within $0.03$ in $g$ over the whole plane
(orange dashed versus black), and for $U\lesssim5t$ it also reproduces $g_c(U)$ to within the grid
($\delta g=0.01$), while underestimating the maximal current by $10$--$20\%$: the product state renormalizes
the junction bond by $|\langle e^{ig(a+a^\dagger)}\rangle|<1$ and loses phase-independent kinetic energy
that the exact state recovers by following the flux adiabatically~\cite{Li2020,Dmytruk2021}. What it
cannot capture is the coherence between the two wells. By construction it breaks the symmetry
(self-trapping) and predicts a current of $0.5$--$0.7\,I_c$ for $U=6$--$8t$, where the exact ground state is
a cat with $\delta\sim10^{-2}$--$10^{-1}\hbar\omega$ that carries no current for any flux offset below
$\delta/I^\star\sim10^{-3}$--$10^{-2}\Phi_0$. The product-state current is therefore meaningful only where
$\delta$ is negligible, $E_J\gtrsim4\hbar\omega$. The same failure, in extreme form, occurs for the parameters of Ref.~\cite{notebook}, a short ring
($L\lesssim\xi$) with a Zeeman-polarized dot and $E_J\simeq\hbar\omega$, $g\simeq1$: mean field predicts a
current above $g\simeq0.75$, whereas the DMRG ground state lies $1.8\hbar\omega$ lower, keeps a gap of
$0.33\hbar\omega$ and carries no current at any $g$~\cite{SM}. The product state is reliable when
$E_J\gg\hbar\omega$ and $g\ll1$, i.e.\ when the mode is nearly classical.

\paragraph*{Experimental scales.}
The two control parameters are $\beta=2\pi LI_c/\Phi_0$ and $E_J/\hbar\omega$, related by
$\beta=2\pi(Z/R_Q)(E_J/\hbar\omega)$, with $E_J/h\simeq0.5\,{\rm GHz}\times I_c[{\rm nA}]$. Three classes of
$\pi$ elements cover Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(b). (i) SFS junctions ($I_c\sim1$--$100\,\mu$A) reach $\beta>1$
with geometric inductances of pH to nH and have $E_J/\hbar\omega\sim10^2$--$10^4$: the classical limit, where
the half-flux state is fully polarized, as observed~\cite{Ryazanov2001,Bauer2004}. (ii) Quantum-dot $\pi$ junctions in the doublet regime ($I_c\sim0.1$--$10$\,nA) need a superinductance
$L\sim0.03$--$3\,\mu$H as in fluxonium~\cite{Manucharyan2009,Grunhaupt2019}, the $\pi$-qubit geometry of
Refs.~\cite{Ioffe1999,Yamashita2005,Feofanov2010}. There $E_J/\hbar\omega\sim1$, the ground state is the cat
with $\delta\sim0.1\hbar\omega$, and the transition appears as a dip of the $|0\rangle\to|1\rangle$
frequency and a jump of $\langle0|\Phi|1\rangle$ in two-tone spectroscopy. (iii) Multichannel or gate-tunable $\pi$ junctions with $I_c\sim50$--$200$\,nA in a
high-kinetic-inductance loop ($L\sim2$--$10$\,nH, $\omega/2\pi\sim2$--$5$\,GHz) give $\beta\sim1$--$3$ and
$E_J/\hbar\omega\sim10$--$30$, the regime of Figs.~\ref{fig:main} and~\ref{fig:udot}: a well-defined
current of $\sim10$ photons whose residual tunnelling $\delta/\hbar\omega\sim10^{-4}$--$10^{-8}$ is a measurable
macroscopic-quantum-tunnelling signature. In a $0$-junction biased by $\Phi_0/2$ the same state exists, but
its direction is selected by the bias, which must then be stable to $|\delta\Phi|\ll\delta/2I^\star\lesssim10^{-6}\Phi_0$;
the interaction-driven realization has no such requirement.

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
A $\pi$ loop whose inductance is a quantum LC mode is the minimal system in which a cavity field condenses
in the ground state. The condensate is the persistent current, its criterion is the classical screening
parameter $\beta>1$ applied to the paramagnetic response of the $\pi$ junction, and its direction is chosen
spontaneously because an interacting dot supplies the $\pi$ shift without breaking time reversal. The
quantum nature of the flux shows up not in the threshold but in the ground state, a cat of the two
currents with a tunnel splitting that vanishes as $e^{-E_J/\hbar\omega}$ and is the only scale between
them. The exact solution also delimits the self-consistent product state: it is trustworthy for
$E_J\gg\hbar\omega$ and fails, by predicting order that is not there, once the mode is quantum. The same
machinery applies to any paramagnetic element in a circuit-QED loop, and the fourfold
$\{\sigma\}\otimes\{\cw,\ccw\}$ multiplet of the dot realization is a natural starting point for a protected
qubit.

\section*{S1. Gauge covariance and the kinetic inductance of the arms}
\emph{Gauge.} With real $\Delta$ on every site, the BdG energy of the ring with the whole Peierls phase
$\varphi$ on the junction bond coincides, to $10^{-10}t$, with that of a uniform vector potential
$a_r=\varphi/L$ on all bonds together with the covariant pair field $\Delta_r=\Delta e^{-2i\theta_r}$,
$\theta_{r+1}-\theta_r=\varphi\delta_{r0}-a_r$. A uniform $a_r$ with real $\Delta$ is not equivalent (at
$\varphi=\pi/2$ it differs by $0.13t$): it describes a condensate whose phase does \emph{not} wind, i.e.\ a
different physical situation. Eq.~(1) is thus the model of a loop in which the condensate phase of the arms
is pinned by a bulk superconductor and the entire gauge-invariant phase drop sits on the junction.

\emph{Kinetic inductance.} If the chain is instead a self-supporting superconductor, $\Delta_r=-U_a\langle
c_{r\downarrow}c_{r\uparrow}\rangle$ with $U_a$ chosen such that $|\Delta|=t$, the phase drop spreads over the
arms. For the weak link ($t_J=0.5t$) only $31\%$ of it sits on the junction, and the curvature of the
self-consistent $E(\varphi)$ obeys the series rule $1/E''_{\rm sc}=1/E''_{\rm pinned}+1/K$ to $6\%$, with a
stiffness of the 13 arm bonds $K=0.080t$, i.e.\ a kinetic inductance $L_K=(\hbar/e)^2/K$. In the loop
energy this inductance is in series with that of the mode, and the instability condition becomes
$\beta+\beta_K=1$ with $\beta_K=4E_J/K=2\pi L_KI_c/\Phi_0$: $0.57$, $0.38$, $0.20$ and $0.12$ at $U=t$, $2t$,
$4t$ and $6t$. In a real device $L_K$ is simply part of $L$; the fixed-$\Delta$ model isolates the
contribution of the mode.

For an ordinary weak link biased by $\varphi_{\rm cl}=\pi/2$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:halfflux}) the current rises
at $\beta\simeq1.1$, saturates at $0.97\,I_c$ near $\beta=1.7$ and then decreases as the flux approaches
$\Phi_0/2$. DMRG and adiabatic theory agree to $0.003\hbar\omega$ in energy, $5\%$ in current and $2\%$ in
$E_1-E_0$; the product state is $0.2$--$0.4\hbar\omega$ too high and overestimates the current threefold
near threshold ($0.71\,I_c$ versus $0.21\,I_c$ at $\beta=1.13$), becoming accurate only deep in the ordered
phase where the photon is nearly coherent. The flux distribution evolves from a Gaussian into a
\emph{single} displaced peak: the residual odd harmonic $E_1=-5.4\times10^{-4}t$ tilts the double well by
$2|E_1|\sin\varphi^\star\approx0.2\hbar\omega$, which fixes the current direction and sets $E_1-E_0$ for
$\beta>1.2$ in DMRG and adiabatic theory alike (without it $\delta/\hbar\omega$ would drop below $10^{-6}$
at $\beta=2$). In a homogeneous ring this tilt is $\propto e^{-L/\xi}$; in practice it is set by the flux
offset.

\section*{S4. Numerical details}
\emph{Current from DMRG.} In the ordered phase the two lowest states of the full problem are degenerate to
$\delta\ll$ the DMRG energy resolution, and the variational ground state is an uncontrolled superposition
of them. One-site observables that are even under time reversal ($\langle a^\dagger a\rangle$,
$\langle X^2\rangle$) and the doublet quantities ($\delta$, $|\langle0|X|1\rangle|$) are therefore robust,
whereas $\langle X\rangle$ and $\langle I\rangle$ of a seeded run are reliable only where the seed energy
$I^\star\varphi_{\rm cl}$ exceeds both $\delta$ and the energy resolution. The three-site bond-current
operator is in addition a small difference of large terms and converges poorly at $\chi=256$; we use
Eq.~(5) throughout. \emph{Harmonics.} In the doublet sector a six-harmonic cosine fit of $E(\varphi)$ on 9--17
phases reproduces the DMRG energies to $10^{-7}t$. The singlet sector has a cusp at $\varphi=\pi/2$ (level
crossing); since this sector is diamagnetic and the photon stays within $|gX|<\pi/2$, it is interpolated on
$[0,\pi/2]$ only. Between the 16 DMRG values of $U$ the harmonics are interpolated with monotone cubic
splines. \emph{Classical line.} $g_c^{\rm cl}(U)=[\hbar\omega/2|E''(0;U)|]^{1/2}$ in the doublet sector.
\emph{DMRG resolution of $\delta$.} At $U=2t$, $\beta=1.5$, DMRG gives $\delta=6\times10^{-6}t$ and
$3\times10^{-5}t$ at $\chi=256$ and $400$, against $5\times10^{-7}t$ from the adiabatic theory; we quote
$\sim3\times10^{-5}t$ as the resolution.
