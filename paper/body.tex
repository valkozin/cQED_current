A superconducting loop closed by a $\pi$ junction carries a spontaneous persistent current when its
inductance exceeds $\Phi_0/2\pi I_c$~\cite{BKS1977,Tsuei2000,Ryazanov2001,Bauer2004}, and with a small
capacitance the two current directions form a flux qubit that needs no external
field~\cite{Ioffe1999,Yamashita2005,Yamashita2006,Kawabata2006,Feofanov2010,Kim2024}. The same circuit is the
counter-example to the no-go theorem for superradiance: a mode that couples through its magnetic flux can
condense a static field $\langle a\rangle\neq0$~\cite{NatafCiuti2010,Viehmann2011,Bamba2016,Jaako2016,Nataf2019,Guerci2020,Andolina2020,RomanRoche2021},
the condition being the magnetostatic instability of the matter~\cite{Andolina2020,Mercurio2024}. In all
of these descriptions the junction is a fixed function $E(\varphi)$, or electrons and mode are decoupled
in a product state~\cite{Varrica2026}. A quantum-dot Josephson junction is not a fixed $E(\varphi)$: it is a
$\pi$ junction in its doublet
state~\cite{GlazmanMatveev1989,SpivakKivelson1991,Vecino2003,Karrasch2008,MengFlorensSimon2009,vanDam2006,Cleuziou2006,Delagrange2015},
its singlet--doublet transition is read out through the frequency of a transmon or
resonator~\cite{Bargerbos2022,Hays2021,PitaVidal2023}, shunted by a superinductor it is a protected spin
qubit~\cite{delOlmo2026,Padurariu2010}, and whether it is $0$ or $\pi$ is decided by $U$, the level width
$\Gamma$ and $\Delta$ on a scale of a few GHz, comparable to the mode frequency. Here we treat the electrons
of the junction and the quantized flux on the same footing and solve the electron--photon problem exactly
by DMRG, as done for normal chains~\cite{Passetti2023,Eckhardt2022} and ladders~\cite{Bacciconi2023} in a
cavity. The zero-point flux of the mode turns out to be a control parameter of the $0$--$\pi$ transition;
deep in the adiabatic regime the loop is exactly a fluxonium with the microscopic $E(\varphi)$; and the
product-state mean field fails where $E_J\lesssim\hbar\omega$ or $U\simeq U_c$.

\paragraph*{Model.}
The circuit of Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(a) is an $s$-wave BCS chain of $L-1$ sites (hopping $t$, pairing
$\Delta$) closed by an Anderson impurity (site $0$; $\epsilon_d=-U/2$, repulsion $U$, tunnelling $t_J$
to both ends) and threaded by the flux of one lumped $LC$ mode:
\begin{align}
  H &= \hbar\omega\, a^\dagger a
     -t\sum_{r=1}^{L-2}\sum_\sigma\left(c^\dagger_{r+1\sigma}c_{r\sigma}+{\rm h.c.}\right)
     \nonumber\\
    &\quad +\Delta\sum_{r=1}^{L-1}\left(c^\dagger_{r\uparrow}c^\dagger_{r\downarrow}+{\rm h.c.}\right)
     +\epsilon_d\, n_0 + U n_{0\uparrow}n_{0\downarrow}
     \nonumber\\
    &\quad -t_J\sum_\sigma\left(c^\dagger_{0\sigma}c_{L-1\,\sigma}+{\rm h.c.}\right)
     \nonumber\\
    &\quad -t_J\sum_\sigma\left(e^{i\varphi_{\rm cl}}\,e^{ig(a+a^\dagger)}\,c^\dagger_{1\sigma}c_{0\sigma}+{\rm h.c.}\right).
  \label{eq:H}
\end{align}
The mode enters through the displacement operator $D=e^{ig(a+a^\dagger)}$, so diamagnetic terms are kept
to all orders~\cite{Li2020,Dmytruk2021}; $g=2\pi\Phi_{\rm zpf}/(h/e)$ is the single-electron phase of the
zero-point flux, $\varphi=\pi$ is one flux quantum $\Phi_0=h/2e$, and for a mode of impedance $Z$ sharing
its inductance with the loop, $g^2=\pi Z/4R_Q$ ($R_Q=h/4e^2$). $\varphi_{\rm cl}$ is a static flux, used
as the $\Phi_0/2$ bias of a weak link or as an infinitesimal seed. Placing the whole phase on the dot
bond is a statement about the circuit, not a gauge choice: a static phase can be spread along the ring by
a gauge transformation with the pairing transformed covariantly~\cite{Dmytruk2021} (Supplemental Material,
SM), but the quantum part cannot~\cite{DmytrukTrifSimon2016}, since moving a fraction $\lambda$ of $gX$ onto
other bonds generates $\hbar\omega(\lambda NP+\lambda^2N^2)$, a capacitive coupling of the displaced charge $N$
to the charge $P$ of the mode. Eq.~\eqref{eq:H} is the circuit in which the voltage of the mode drops across
the junction, the only high-impedance element of the loop; the omitted terms are of relative order
$\hbar\omega g^2/E_A$. Unless stated otherwise, $\Delta=t$ (energies in $\Delta$, currents in $e\Delta/\hbar$),
$t_J=0.5\Delta$ ($\Gamma=2t_J^2/t=\Delta/2$), $L=10>\xi\simeq t/\Delta$ (finite-size and $h/e$
corrections~\cite{LossGoldbart1991} are $\le2\%$, SM), $\varphi_{\rm cl}=0$.

Fermion parity $s$ is conserved, so the ground-state energy of the ring at fixed phase,
$E_s(\varphi)=\sum_mE^{(s)}_m\cos m\varphi$, is defined sector by sector: the singlet (even) sector has
$E_2<0$ (a $0$ junction), the doublet (odd) sector $E_2>0$ (a $\pi$ junction), and $E_s(\varphi)=E_s(-\varphi)$
exactly because $H[\varphi_{\rm cl}=0]$ is real. The persistent current is
$I=-(e/\hbar)\partial H/\partial\varphi_{\rm cl}$, the particle current through the dot bond dressed by $D$,
and $\langle[H,P]\rangle=0$ gives the sum rule $\langle I\rangle=(e/\hbar)(\hbar\omega/2g)\langle a+a^\dagger\rangle$
in every eigenstate: the current \emph{is} the static displacement of the mode, and the difference of the two
sides is our acceptance test for every DMRG state.

\paragraph*{Two limits.}
If $\hbar\omega$ is small compared with the excitation gap $E_A$ of the ring at fixed phase and parity,
the electrons follow the flux adiabatically and, in each sector,
\begin{equation}
  H^{(s)}_{\rm BO}=\frac{\hbar\omega}{4}\left(P^2+X^2\right)+E_s(\varphi_{\rm cl}+gX),\qquad [X,P]=2i,
  \label{eq:BO}
\end{equation}
which in the Cooper-pair phase $\theta=2gX$ is $4E_Cn^2+\tfrac12E_L\theta^2+E_s(\theta/2)$ with
$E_L=\hbar\omega/8g^2$, $E_C=\hbar\omega g^2$: the rf-SQUID of Refs.~\cite{Friedman2000,Kawabata2006}, a
fluxonium~\cite{Manucharyan2009} whose Josephson element is the dot. A static displacement appears
classically when the paramagnetic response of a $\pi$ loop exceeds the stiffness of the mode,
\begin{equation}
  \beta\equiv\frac{2g^2|E''(0)|}{\hbar\omega}=\frac{8g^2E_J}{\hbar\omega}=\frac{2\pi LI_c}{\Phi_0}>1,
  \label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
the screening condition of Refs.~\cite{BKS1977,Tsuei2000}, the superradiance condition of the circuit of
Bamba \emph{et al.}~\cite{Bamba2016} and the single-loop form of the magnetostatic criterion of
Refs.~\cite{Andolina2020,RomanRoche2021,Mercurio2024}. Zero-point fluctuations oppose the
condensation~\cite{BambaImoto2017}: at finite $E_J/\hbar\omega$ the ground state is the symmetric
superposition of the two current states, split by $\delta\sim\hbar\omega e^{-S}$ with $S\propto E_J/\hbar\omega$,
and a sharp transition exists only for $E_J/\hbar\omega\to\infty$ at fixed $\beta$~\cite{Hwang2015}. In the
opposite limit, $\hbar\omega\gg E_A$, the mode averages the Peierls factor over its vacuum: a Lang--Firsov
transformation~\cite{LangFirsov1963} gives $H_{\rm ring}$ with $t_J\to t_Je^{-g^2/2}$ plus $O(t_J^2/\hbar\omega)$,
i.e.\ the zero-point flux reduces the level width of the dot by the Franck--Condon factor $e^{-g^2}$.
Neither limit is assumed below.

\paragraph*{Methods.}
We solve Eq.~\eqref{eq:H} by two-site DMRG (TeNPy~\cite{TeNPy}) on the full electron--photon space: the
ring in folded order next to a bosonic site (Fock cutoff $20$--$62$, last Fock weight $<10^{-7}$), parity
and $S^z$ conserved, $\chi=200$--$400$; benchmarks against BdG ($10^{-14}$) and against exact diagonalization
of a four-site ring with a photon, sum rule included ($10^{-7}$). Seeded scans use two initial states
(vacuum side, polarized side) and seed annealing, because the two current states are split only by
$\delta$ and by $2I^\star\varphi_{\rm cl}\sim10^{-5}\Delta$ (SM). We compare with (i) Eq.~\eqref{eq:BO}
diagonalized exactly with the DMRG $E_s(\varphi)$ (``circuit model''), (ii) the Lang--Firsov ring, and
(iii) the self-consistent electron$\times$photon product state (Hartree--Fock BdG electrons times a photon
state, ``mean field'', MF), the ansatz of Refs.~\cite{Varrica2026,Andolina2020} for this circuit.
