\paragraph*{Model.}
We consider a superconducting (SC) loop of $L$ sites closed by an interacting
quantum dot (site $0$) and threaded by the magnetic flux of a single lumped LC mode
[Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(a)]. The full microscopic Hamiltonian is
\begin{align}
  H &= \hbar\omega\, a^\dagger a
     -t\sum_{r=1}^{L-2}\sum_\sigma\left(c^\dagger_{r+1\sigma}c_{r\sigma}+{\rm h.c.}\right)
     \nonumber\\
    &\quad +\Delta\sum_{r=1}^{L-1}\left(c^\dagger_{r\uparrow}c^\dagger_{r\downarrow}+{\rm h.c.}\right)
     +\epsilon_d\, n_0 + U n_{0\uparrow}n_{0\downarrow}
     \nonumber\\
    &\quad -t_J\sum_\sigma\left(c^\dagger_{0\sigma}c_{L-1\,\sigma}+{\rm h.c.}\right)
     \nonumber\\
    &\quad -t_J\sum_\sigma\left(e^{i\varphi_{\rm cl}}\,e^{ig(a+a^\dagger)}\,c^\dagger_{1\sigma}c_{0\sigma}+{\rm h.c.}\right).
  \label{eq:H}
\end{align}
The first two lines are the resonator, an $s$-wave BCS chain (hopping $t$, pairing $\Delta$)
and the Anderson impurity (level $\epsilon_d=-U/2$, on-site
repulsion $U$) tunnel-coupled ($t_J$) to the two ends of the chain; the last line
carries the total Peierls phase of the loop, which by a gauge choice is placed on a
single bond. The vector potential of the mode enters through the gauge-invariant
displacement operator $D=e^{ig(a+a^\dagger)}$, so diamagnetic ($A^2$) terms are
included to all orders; $g=2\pi\Phi_{\rm zpf}/(h/e)$ is the electron phase generated
by the zero-point flux of the mode, and $\varphi_{\rm cl}$ is a static external
flux. An electron phase $\varphi=\pi$ corresponds to one superconducting flux
quantum $\Phi_0=h/2e$. For a lumped resonator of impedance $Z$, galvanically sharing
its inductance with the loop, $(2g)^2=\pi Z/R_Q$ with $R_Q=h/4e^2$. Unless stated
otherwise we take $\Delta=t$ and quote energies in units of $\Delta$ (currents in units
of $e\Delta/\hbar$), $t_J=0.5\Delta$ (level width $\Gamma=2t_J^2/t=\Delta/2$), $L=10$,
$\hbar\omega=7.39\times10^{-4}\Delta$ and \emph{no} external flux, $\varphi_{\rm cl}\to0^+$
(a seed $\varphi_{\rm cl}=10^{-3}$ is used to select one of the degenerate states).
The ring is longer than the coherence length $\xi\simeq t/\Delta$: the $\pi$-sector Josephson
energy of the $U=2\Delta$ junction, $E_2=7.39\times10^{-3}\Delta$, changes by $0.2\%$ between
$L=8$ and $L=14$, and its single-electron harmonic is $|E_1|/E_2\le2\%$. Two auxiliary
versions are also used: a weak link ($U=0$, dot replaced by a bond $t_J$) biased by
$\Phi_0/2$, for which we take $L=14$ because the single-electron harmonic of a homogeneous
ring decays only as $e^{-L/\xi}$ ($|E_1/E_2|=1\%$ at $L=14$ against $8\%$ at $L=10$), and the
short ring with a Zeeman-polarized dot of Ref.~\cite{notebook}.
We write Eq.~\eqref{eq:H} compactly as
$H=\hbar\omega a^\dagger a+H_{\rm ring}[\varphi_{\rm cl}+g(a+a^\dagger)]$.

The only property of $H_{\rm ring}$ that enters the adiabatic theory below is its ground-state
energy at fixed phase and fixed fermion parity $s$,
\begin{equation}
  E_s(\varphi)=\sum_{m\ge 0}E_m\cos m\varphi .
  \label{eq:harm}
\end{equation}
Even harmonics ($m=2,4,\dots$) describe Cooper-pair tunnelling ($h/2e$
periodicity). The $m=1$ term is the single-electron ($h/e$) persistent
current of a mesoscopic ring~\cite{LossGoldbart1991} and is suppressed as $e^{-L/\xi}$. A
$0$-junction has $E_2<0$ and a $\pi$-junction has $E_2>0$. When a $0$-junction
is biased by $\Phi_0/2$, $\varphi_{\rm cl}=\pi/2$ maps $E_2\cos2\varphi\to-E_2\cos2\varphi$.
The two routes to a $\pi$ loop are therefore equivalent up to the odd harmonics,
which turn into an explicit symmetry-breaking term
$-E_1\sin\varphi$. In contrast, an intrinsic $\pi$-junction keeps
$E(\varphi)=E(-\varphi)$ exactly, since $H_{\rm ring}[0]$ is real
(time-reversal symmetry).

\paragraph*{Gauge-invariant current and sum rule.}
Since the Peierls phase of the loop is placed on the dot--ring bond, the persistent
current is the derivative of $H$ with respect to the total flux,
$I=-\partial H/\partial\Phi=-(e/\hbar)\,\partial H/\partial\varphi_{\rm cl}$ (the Peierls phase is the
single-electron phase, $\Phi=\hbar\varphi/e$),
\begin{equation}
  I=\frac{e}{\hbar}\,i\,t_J\sum_\sigma\!\left(e^{i\varphi_{\rm cl}}e^{ig(a+a^\dagger)}c^\dagger_{1\sigma}c_{0\sigma}
  -{\rm h.c.}\right)
  \label{eq:Iop}
\end{equation}
(in terms of the Cooper-pair phase $\theta=2\varphi$ the same operator reads
$-(2e/\hbar)\partial H/\partial\theta$). This is the particle current through the
dot--ring bond, dressed by the displacement operator of the mode: the combination
$e^{i(\varphi_{\rm cl}+gX)}c^\dagger_1c_0$ is invariant under the simultaneous
gauge transformation of the electrons and of the vector potential, and its
expectation value does not change if the Peierls phase is distributed along the
ring with the pairing amplitude transformed covariantly~\cite{Dmytruk2021}.
Because the dot carries no pairing term, the particle number of site $0$ is conserved by
$H$ and the current entering the dot from site $L-1$ equals the current~\eqref{eq:Iop}
leaving it; the same is not true of the bonds inside the BCS chain, where the
fixed-phase pairing term acts as a condensate reservoir~\cite{Dmytruk2021}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:Iop} obeys an exact sum rule. With $X=a+a^\dagger$ and
$P=-i(a-a^\dagger)$, $[H,P]=i\hbar\omega X-2ig\,(\hbar/e)I$, so that in any eigenstate
\begin{equation}
  \langle I\rangle=\frac{e}{\hbar}\,\frac{\hbar\omega}{2g}\,\langle a+a^\dagger\rangle :
  \label{eq:sumrule}
\end{equation}
the persistent current \emph{is} the static displacement of the mode (the dc flux
it generates, $\Phi=LI$). The two sides are a fermionic and a photonic observable, and
only an eigenstate satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:sumrule}; we evaluate both independently and use
their difference as the acceptance test of every DMRG state (Methods).

\paragraph*{Adiabatic reduction and Landau theory.}
For $\hbar\omega$ well below the Andreev gap $E_A$, the electrons follow the
flux adiabatically. To leading order in $\hbar\omega/E_A$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:H}
reduces, in each parity sector, to a single-particle problem for the dimensionless flux $X=a+a^\dagger$,
\begin{equation}
  H_{\rm BO}=\frac{\hbar\omega}{4}\left(P^2+X^2\right)+E_s(\varphi_{\rm cl}+gX),
  \qquad [X,P]=2i .
  \label{eq:BO}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:BO} is the Hamiltonian of an rf-SQUID~\cite{Friedman2000} whose inductive energy
is supplied by the mode. Writing it in the Cooper-pair phase
$\theta=2gX$ gives $4E_C n^2+\tfrac12E_L\theta^2+E(\theta/2)$ with
$E_L=\hbar\omega/8g^2$ and $E_C=\hbar\omega g^2$. A static field ($\langle a\rangle\neq0$),
i.e.\ a photon condensate carrying a persistent current
$I=-(e/\hbar)\partial_\varphi E$, appears classically when the orbital response of the
ring at the symmetric point is paramagnetic and exceeds the stiffness of the mode,
\begin{equation}
  \beta\equiv\frac{2g^2\,|E''(\varphi_0)|}{\hbar\omega}>1,
  \qquad E''(\varphi_0)<0 .
  \label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
For a pure $\pi$-junction, $\beta=8g^2E_J/\hbar\omega=2\pi L I_c/\Phi_0$ with $E_J\equiv E_2$, the
familiar screening parameter of the loop \cite{BKS1977,Tsuei2000}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:beta} is the single-loop form of the
photon-condensation criterion of Andolina \emph{et al.}~\cite{Andolina2020}: the no-go theorem
for a uniform cavity field~\cite{Andolina2019,Nataf2019} does not apply, because the mode couples to the
flux (curl of $\mathbf A$), and the instability requires a \emph{paramagnetic}
orbital response, which here comes from the $\pi$ shift.
Above threshold, the Landau free energy
$\mathcal F(X)=\hbar\omega X^2/4+E(gX)$ has two degenerate minima
$\pm X^\star$ carrying opposite currents $\pm I^\star$. Below threshold, in a $0$ loop
($E''(0)>0$), the same expansion gives a mode that is not softened but stiffened,
$\omega\to\omega'=\omega\sqrt{1+2g^2E''(0)/\hbar\omega}$, whose ground state is a squeezed
vacuum with $\langle a^\dagger a\rangle=(\omega'/\omega+\omega/\omega')/4-1/2$ virtual photons.

Two remarks fix the physical content of Eq.~\eqref{eq:beta}. First,
for a loop with its own geometric inductance $L_r$ and mutual inductance
$M\le\sqrt{L_rL_c}$ to the resonator, minimizing the static energy over the
resonator flux gives $\Phi_r^2/2L_r$: the static threshold is set by the
total loop inductance, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:H} is the $k=M/\sqrt{L_rL_c}\to1$
(galvanic) limit. Second, zero-point fluctuations of the mode do not
drive the transition; they oppose it. At finite $E_J/\hbar\omega$ the
ground state of Eq.~\eqref{eq:BO} is the symmetric superposition of the two
current-carrying states, split from its partner by a tunnelling gap
$\delta\sim\hbar\omega\,e^{-S}$ with an instanton action
$S\propto (E_J/\hbar\omega)\,s(\beta)$. In the exact solution below this is
quantitative: the threshold lies \emph{above} the classical line $\beta=1$ by
$\Delta g=0.02$, $0.04$ and $0.12$ as $E_J/\hbar\omega$ falls from $10$ to $5$ and $3.2$,
the crossover broadens from one grid step to $\Delta g\simeq0.08$ at $E_J/\hbar\omega=3.2$,
and below $E_J/\hbar\omega\simeq2.5$ no ordered phase is left for $g\le0.4$.
A sharp quantum phase transition
with mean-field exponents is recovered only in the scaling limit
$E_J/\hbar\omega\to\infty$ at fixed $\beta$, in which
$E_C/E_J=\beta(\hbar\omega)^2/(8E_J^2)\to0$. This is the analogue of the
classical-oscillator limit of the quantum Rabi model~\cite{Hwang2015}.

\paragraph*{Methods.}
We solve Eq.~\eqref{eq:H} without approximation by two-site DMRG
(TeNPy~\cite{TeNPy}) on the full electron--photon Hilbert space. The ring is stored in folded
order next to a bosonic site (Fock cutoff $N_{\rm ph}=50$--$62$, chosen such that the last Fock state
carries a weight $<10^{-7}$). Fermion parity and $S^z$
are conserved, and the electron--photon vertex is a three-site operator
$D\,c^\dagger_{1\sigma}c_{0\sigma}$. Benchmarks against exact BdG energies of the
non-interacting ring at $g=0$ agree to $10^{-14}$ at full bond dimension
($\chi=1024$); for a $4$-site ring with a photon, DMRG reproduces exact diagonalization and the
sum rule~\eqref{eq:sumrule} to $10^{-7}$. In production $\chi=300$ (truncation error $<2\times10^{-8}$;
$\chi=400$ changes the energies by $<4\times10^{-8}\Delta$ and the current by $<0.3\%$ away from
the crossover points). The accuracy of the energy is, however, not the relevant criterion.
The two current-carrying states are split only by the exponentially small $\delta$ and by the
seed, $2I^\star\varphi_{\rm cl}\sim10^{-5}\Delta$, so that the energy landscape between the vacuum
and the polarized state is flat on the scale of $10^{-7}$--$10^{-6}\Delta$ that decides the
polarization, while the local updates of DMRG drift towards the polarized state over tens of
sweeps and can stop in an under-polarized state whose energy is within $10^{-6}\Delta$ of
the ground state and whose current is several times too small. An earlier version of our
scans suffered from this and, in addition, evaluated the observables after a warm start with the
displacement operator of the previous $g$: the apparent poor convergence of the bond-current
operator, which we had attributed to a cancellation of large terms, was this error, and the
sum rule~\eqref{eq:sumrule} exposes both. All DMRG results below therefore follow one protocol.
(i) Every point is started from two independent states: a warm start from the converged
neighbouring point on the vacuum side (A), and a product state with the mode in a coherent
state at the classical flux, converged first with a larger seed ($\varphi_{\rm cl}=10^{-2}$,
then $3\times10^{-3}$) and then relaxed at the final seed (C), which approaches the ground
state from the polarized side. (ii) The lower energy is kept whenever the two differ by more
than $3\times10^{-7}\Delta$; otherwise the pair is reported as a bracket. (iii) A state is
accepted only if it satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:sumrule} to better than $10^{-3}I_c$ (median
$10^{-4}I_c$ over all points with $g\ge0.04$). (iv) The current above threshold must be
independent of the seed. Excited states are obtained by DMRG orthogonal to the ground state.
We compare with (i) the self-consistent electron$\times$photon product state (mean field, MF),
the method of Ref.~\cite{notebook} generalized to the interacting dot (Hartree--Fock BdG
electrons times a photon product state, swept upwards in $g$ with the same seed), and (ii) the
adiabatic Hamiltonian~\eqref{eq:BO}, with $E_s(\varphi)$ from BdG or from DMRG at fixed
$\varphi$ and $g=0$, diagonalized exactly in a Fock space of $200$ states.
