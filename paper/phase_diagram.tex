\paragraph*{Persistent-current phase of the $\pi$ loop.}
We now take $\hbar\omega=E_J(U=2\Delta)/10=7.39\times10^{-4}\Delta\ll E_A$, so that the classical line
$\beta=1$ sweeps across $g\le0.4$ ($Z\le1.3\,{\rm k}\Omega$) as $U$ runs from $U_c$ to $8\Delta$ while
$E_J/\hbar\omega$ falls from $15$ to $2$, and compute the ground state on a grid of $427$ points
($g=0.02$--$0.40$, $14$ values of $U$, both sectors for $U\le\Delta$, seed $10^{-3}$, $\chi=300$).
Figure~\ref{fig:phasemain} shows the seeded persistent current of the full DMRG (a), the seed-free order
parameter of the circuit model, the tunnel splitting $\delta(U,g)$ between the two current directions (b),
and the mean field (c); cuts are given in the SM.

Three regions appear. (i) For $U<U_c(g)$ the loop is a $0$ loop: the singlet is the ground state, the
response is diamagnetic and the mode is stiffened, $\omega'=\omega\sqrt{1+2g^2E''(0)/\hbar\omega}$, up to
$9.4\omega$ at $g=0.4$, with the squeezed vacuum of the stiffened mode as ground state (its photon number
$(\omega'/\omega+\omega/\omega')/4-\tfrac12$ reproduces the DMRG value, $1.89$ against $1.91$ at $g=0.4$).
This is the inductive frequency shift that reads out the parity of dot junctions~\cite{Bargerbos2022,Hays2021}.
The boundary is the adiabatic end of Fig.~\ref{fig:cavity0pi}: it moves from $U_c^0=0.94\Delta$ at
$g\to0$ to $0.85\Delta$ at $g=0.4$, the crossing of the \emph{global} minima of the two surfaces,
$E_{\rm odd}(\pi/2)=E_{\rm even}(0)$. The window between is the $\pi'$ phase of the
junction~\cite{Vecino2003,Karrasch2008}, singlet at zero flux but doublet at $\Phi_0/2$, and a loop whose
flux is free to adjust switches to the doublet by letting $\Phi_0/2$ in, at $g\simeq0.3$ for $U=0.9\Delta$.
(ii) For $U>U_c$ and $g>g_c(U)$ the mode condenses: at $U=2\Delta$ the photon number rises from $0.5$ at
$g=0.11$ to $7.3$ at $g=0.14$, where the current reaches $0.96I_c$, and then falls as
$I\simeq(e/\hbar)\hbar\omega\varphi^\star/2g^2$ because the flux saturates at $\Phi_0/2$ while the inductance
$\propto g^2/\hbar\omega$ grows. The threshold ($|\langle I\rangle|=0.5I_c$) lies \emph{above} $\beta=1$ by
$\Delta g=0.02$, $0.04$ and $0.12$ at $U=2,4,6\Delta$ ($E_J/\hbar\omega=10$, $5$, $3.2$): the zero-point
motion of the flux opposes the condensation, and the crossover broadens from one grid step to
$\Delta g\simeq0.08$. (iii) At $U=8\Delta$ ($E_J/\hbar\omega=2.1$) no ordered phase is left for $g\le0.4$
although $\beta$ reaches $2.7$: the ground state is the cat state with $\delta\simeq\hbar\omega$ and a current
$\le0.04I_c$ proportional to the seed. Seeded currents are a crossover diagnostic at finite
$E_J/\hbar\omega$; the seed-free map (b) has the same boundary as the contour $\delta=0.1\hbar\omega$ and shows
the exponential collapse of $\delta$ inside the ordered region.

Above threshold the full DMRG and the circuit model agree to $0.2\%$ of $I_c$ on average ($2.4\%$ at
worst), to $0.1$ in the photon number and to $10^{-5}\Delta$ in the doublet-sector energies: at
$\hbar\omega/E_A\sim10^{-3}$ the elimination of the fermions is exact for all practical purposes and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:BO} with the microscopic $E_s(\varphi)$ is the correct circuit model. Mean field locates $g_c$
to within the grid step for $U\le5\Delta$ but underestimates the current by $10$--$20\%$ (the product state
renormalizes the junction bond by $|\langle D\rangle|<1$), overestimates the photon number at large $g$ ($5.2$
against $3.7$ at $U=2\Delta$, $g=0.4$), predicts an ordered phase with $0.6I_c$ at $U=7$--$8\Delta$ that does
not exist, and, with its Hartree--Fock boundary $U_c^{\rm MF}=0.82\Delta$, orders the loop at $U=0.85\Delta$ with
$0.95I_c$ where the exact ground state is the singlet $0$ loop. Ref.~\cite{Varrica2026} applies this ansatz to the same circuit; the
exact solution limits its validity to $E_J\gg\hbar\omega$ and $U$ well above $U_c$, and for $E_J\simeq\hbar\omega$
the time-reversal-breaking state it predicts is absent (SM).
