\paragraph*{Choice of regime and dimensionless variables.}
The microscopic model~\eqref{eq:H} contains the energy scales $t,\Delta,t_J,U$
of the junction and $\hbar\omega,g$ of the mode. Three dimensionless ratios
control the physics. (i) $U/\Delta$ at fixed $\Gamma/\Delta$ 
sets the magnetic state of the dot and hence the sign of the Josephson coupling:
the loop is a $\pi$ loop only for $U>U_c$. (ii) The coupling $g$ is fixed by
the impedance of the mode alone, $g^2=\pi Z/4R_Q$, i.e.\ $g=0.1$--$0.4$
for $Z=80\,\Omega$--$1.3\,{\rm k}\Omega$, from ordinary lumped resonators to the high-kinetic-inductance
(superinducting) ones~\cite{Manucharyan2009,Grunhaupt2019}.
(iii) $E_J/\hbar\omega$, with $E_J\equiv E_2>0$ the Josephson energy of the $\pi$ junction,
decides whether the transition is sharp: the two current states tunnel with
$\delta\sim\hbar\omega e^{-S}$, $S\propto E_J/\hbar\omega$. We take
$\hbar\omega=E_J(U=2\Delta)/10=7.39\times10^{-4}\Delta$. In physical units this is the regime of
a multichannel or gate-tunable $\pi$ junction with $I_c\simeq100$\,nA ($E_J/h\simeq50$\,GHz)
in a loop that shares its inductance with a $5$\,GHz mode: $g=0.1$--$0.4$ then corresponds to a loop
inductance $L=Z/\omega=3$--$40$\,nH, and the screening condition $\beta=2\pi LI_c/\Phi_0=1$ is met at
$L=\Phi_0/2\pi I_c=3.3$\,nH, i.e.\ at $g=0.11$ [class (iii) of the platforms discussed at the end].
With this choice the classical threshold
$\beta=8g^2E_J(U)/\hbar\omega=1$ lies at $g=0.11$ for $U=2\Delta$ and
sweeps across the experimentally accessible window $g\le0.4$ as $U$ is varied
from $U_c$ to $8\Delta$, while $E_J/\hbar\omega$ decreases from $15$ to $2$, so that
the same diagram contains the sharp transition and its quantum washing-out. We present it in the
plane $(U/\Delta,g)$; the right axis of Fig.~\ref{fig:phaseUG} converts $g$ to $Z/R_Q$.

\paragraph*{Grid.}
The grid is $g=0.02$--$0.40$ in steps of $0.01$ ($0.02$ above $0.3$) and
$U/\Delta=0,0.5,0.75,0.85,1,1.25,1.5,2,2.5,3,4,5,6,8$, $427$ ground states in all (about $20$ CPU hours),
each obtained with the two-start protocol of the Methods ($\chi=300$, seed $\varphi_{\rm cl}=10^{-3}$).
For $U\le\Delta$ both parity sectors are computed and the lower one is
taken, which locates the $0$--$\pi$ boundary of the \emph{loop} without any assumption.
The two starts differ by less than $3\times10^{-7}\Delta$ in energy at $47$ points ($11\%$ of the grid, all inside the
crossover windows discussed below), which are reported as brackets
[Fig.~\ref{fig:phaseconv}(b)]. The sum rule~\eqref{eq:sumrule} is satisfied to better than
$10^{-3}I_c$ at every point with $g\ge0.04$ (the worst points are the first ones of each
scan at $g\le0.03$, where the current itself is $<10^{-2}I_c$) [Fig.~\ref{fig:phaseconv}(a)].
Increasing $\chi$ to $400$ changes the current by $<0.3\%$
except at the crossover point; raising $N_{\rm ph}$ to $80$ changes the energy by $<10^{-9}\Delta$; and
reducing the seed to $10^{-4}$ leaves the current above threshold unchanged to $0.4\%$ while the
current below threshold scales down by the factor $0.09$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:phaseconv}(c)]: the
current is spontaneous.

\paragraph*{Effective circuit model.}
When $\hbar\omega$ is small compared to the Andreev gap $E_A$ of the junction
($\hbar\omega/E_A\simeq10^{-3}$ here), the fermions can be integrated out
adiabatically: the mode moves in the exact ground-state energy of the ring at fixed phase and
fixed parity, $H^{(s)}_{\rm eff}=\hbar\omega a^\dagger a+E_s(\varphi_{\rm cl}+gX;U)$, $s={\rm even,odd}$
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:BO}], an rf-SQUID Hamiltonian whose inductive energy is supplied by the mode;
parity conservation forbids any mixing of the two surfaces, and the ground state is the lower
of the two sector ground states. The matching of parameters is unambiguous:
$E_s(\varphi;U)$ is computed by DMRG at $g=0$. The doublet surface is represented by
four harmonics (nine phases); the singlet surface, because of its cusp at $\varphi=\pi/2$
[Fig.~\ref{fig:junctions}(a)], is represented by a spline through $25$ phases. $H_{\rm eff}$ is then
diagonalized exactly in a Fock space of $200$ states, with the same seed. Its current is
$I_{\rm eff}=-(e/\hbar)\partial_\varphi E_s$, the classical current of the junction averaged over the flux
distribution of the mode; the omitted terms are the non-adiabatic (Berry) corrections of relative
order $\hbar\omega/E_A$. The mean-field theory of Ref.~\cite{notebook}, generalized to the
interacting dot (Hartree--Fock BdG electrons $\times$ photon product state, swept upwards in $g$
with the same seed), is evaluated on the same grid.

\paragraph*{Phase diagram.}
Figure~\ref{fig:phaseUG} shows $|\langle I\rangle|/I_c(U)$, with $I_c(U)$ the critical current of
the $\pi$ junction at $g=0$, from the full DMRG (a), the effective circuit model (b) and mean field (c);
Fig.~\ref{fig:phaseslices} shows cuts. The exact diagram has three regions.
(i) For $U<U_c(g)$ the loop is a $0$ loop: the singlet is the ground state at all $g$, the orbital response is
diamagnetic and the mode is not softened but stiffened, $\omega\to\omega'=\omega\sqrt{1+2g^2E''(0)/\hbar\omega}$
with $E''(0)=0.20\Delta$, i.e.\ $\omega'=9.4\,\omega$ at $g=0.4$. The ground state is the squeezed vacuum of the
stiffened mode: its photon number $(\omega'/\omega+\omega/\omega')/4-1/2$ reproduces the DMRG value at every $g$
($1.89$ versus $1.91$ virtual photons at $g=0.4$), while $|\langle I\rangle|<10^{-2}I_c$ and linear in the seed. The boundary is not the zero-flux $0$--$\pi$ crossing of the junction,
$U_c^0=0.94\Delta$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:junctions}(b)]: the doublet gains the condensation energy of the $\pi$ loop
(up to $2E_J$), so that $U_c(g)$ decreases from $0.94\Delta$ at $g\to0$ towards the crossing of the global
minima, $0.85\Delta$, which it reaches at $g\simeq0.4$ [at $U=0.85\Delta$, $E_{\rm odd}-E_{\rm even}$
falls from $2.3\times10^{-2}\Delta$ at $g=0.02$ to $9\times10^{-4}\Delta$ at $g=0.4$]. The flux of the mode thus drives a
parity switch of the junction, and in the window $0.85\Delta<U<0.94\Delta$, where the global minimum
of $E(\varphi)$ already belongs to the doublet, the switch occurs at a finite coupling
($g\simeq0.3$ at $U=0.9\Delta$ in the effective model). This shift of $U_c$ by $0.09\Delta$ is a
prediction specific to the microscopic treatment: the effective circuit model reproduces it only once
the singlet surface, cusp included, is represented exactly.
(ii) For $U>U_c$ and $g>g_c(U)$ the mode condenses: at $U=2\Delta$ the photon number rises from
$0.5$ at $g=0.11$ to $5.7$ at $g=0.13$ and $7.3$ at $g=0.14$, where the current reaches
$0.96\,I_c$; it then decreases as $I\simeq(e/\hbar)\hbar\omega\varphi^\star/2g^2$, because the condensate
flux saturates at $\Phi_0/2$ while the inductance $\propto g^2/\hbar\omega$ grows [Eq.~\eqref{eq:sumrule}].
The threshold $g_c(U)$ (first grid point with $|\langle I\rangle|>0.5I_c$) rises from $0.10$ at $U=\Delta$ to
$0.13$, $0.19$, $0.24$ and $0.32$ at $U=2,4,5,6\Delta$, following the decrease of $E_J(U)$. It lies
systematically \emph{above} the classical line $\beta=1$ (dash-dotted), by $\Delta g=0.02$ at
$U=2\Delta$, $0.04$ at $4\Delta$ and $0.12$ at $6\Delta$: the zero-point motion of the flux opposes
the condensation, and the shift grows as $E_J/\hbar\omega$ decreases from $10$ to $3.2$. At the same
time the transition broadens: the width of the crossover in $g$ grows from one grid step at $U\le3\Delta$
to $0.05$ at $U=5\Delta$ and $0.08$ at $U=6\Delta$, and the maximal current drops from $0.97I_c$ ($U=\Delta$)
to $0.90$, $0.81$ and $0.61I_c$ ($U=4,5,6\Delta$). For $U=8\Delta$ ($E_J/\hbar\omega=2.1$) no ordered
phase is left in the window $g\le0.4$ although $\beta$ reaches $2.7$: there the ground state is the
cat state with $\delta\simeq\hbar\omega$ and $\langle I\rangle\le0.04I_c$, proportional to the seed. This is the
finite-$E_J/\hbar\omega$ remnant of the transition, which becomes sharp only in the limit
$E_J/\hbar\omega\to\infty$ at fixed $\beta$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).

\paragraph*{Comparison with the effective model and with mean field.}
Above threshold the full DMRG and the effective circuit model agree to $0.2\%$ of $I_c$ on average
and to $2.4\%$ at worst over the whole ordered region, and to $0.1$ in the photon number
(the doublet-sector energies agree to better than $10^{-5}\Delta$ at all $g$, to $10^{-6}\Delta$ for $g\le0.3$), which validates the adiabatic elimination
of the fermions at $\hbar\omega/E_A\sim10^{-3}$. The only points where they differ by more are the
bracket points inside the crossover windows, where the exact ground state is a partially polarized
cat and the DMRG from the two initial states brackets the effective-model value from both sides
[vertical bars in Fig.~\ref{fig:phaseslices}(a)]. This is a statement about the convergence of a
variational method on a flat energy landscape, not about the physics: the energy difference between
the two starts is $<3\times10^{-7}\Delta$, while the effective model, which resolves it exactly,
interpolates smoothly between them. Mean field locates $g_c(U)$ to within the grid step for
$U\le5\Delta$ ($0.27$ versus $0.32$ at $6\Delta$) but underestimates the current by $10$--$20\%$
($0.86$ versus $0.96\,I_c$ at $U=2\Delta$; $0.72$ versus $0.90\,I_c$ at $4\Delta$) because the product
state renormalizes the junction bond by $|\langle D\rangle|<1$; it overestimates the photon number at
large $g$ ($5.2$ versus $3.7$ at $U=2\Delta$, $g=0.4$), where the exact flux distribution is a displaced
squeezed state rather than a coherent state; it predicts an ordered phase with $0.6I_c$ at $U=7$--$8\Delta$
that the exact solution does not have, because a product state cannot describe the tunnelling between
the two current directions that restores the symmetry when $E_J\lesssim3\hbar\omega$; and, since
its Hartree--Fock threshold $U_c^{\rm MF}=0.82\Delta$ replaces the Kramers doublet by a static moment,
it orders the loop at $U=0.85\Delta$ with $0.95I_c$ where the exact ground state is the singlet $0$ loop.
In short, mean field gives the right $g_c$ and a $10$--$20\%$ low current where $E_J\gg\hbar\omega$ and
$U$ is well above $U_c$, and fails qualitatively at both ends of the diagram, $U\gtrsim7\Delta$ and
$U\simeq U_c$.
