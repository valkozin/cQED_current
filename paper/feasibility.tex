\paragraph*{Where to look.}
The flux-driven $0$--$\pi$ switch of Fig.~\ref{fig:cavity0pi} needs $\hbar\omega=0.03$--$0.3\Delta$ and
$g=0.3$--$0.6$: for Al ($\Delta=180\,\mu$eV) a mode at $1$--$13$\,GHz sharing its inductance with the loop
and an impedance $Z=4R_Qg^2/\pi=0.7$--$3\,{\rm k}\Omega$, the range of granular-Al or junction-array
superinductors~\cite{Manucharyan2009,Grunhaupt2019}, with the dot gate tuned to within $\sim0.1\Delta$ of
its singlet--doublet boundary as in Refs.~\cite{Bargerbos2022,PitaVidal2023}. It is read out as the jump
of the resonator frequency between the stiffened $0$ phase and the softened $\pi$ phase, and its
signature is that it is driven by the impedance (the superinductor length) at fixed gate voltage.

The persistent-current phase and the cat state require $E_J/\hbar\omega\gtrsim5$ and $\hbar\omega>k_BT$,
which is restrictive: the junction of the phase diagram ($U=2\Delta$, $\Gamma=\Delta/2$,
$E_J=7.4\times10^{-3}\Delta$) has $E_J/h=0.3$\,GHz and $I_c=0.65$\,nA in Al, so $\hbar\omega=E_J/10$ would be
$32$\,MHz, far below the electron temperature. A stronger junction ($\Gamma=2\Delta$, $U=6\Delta$,
$E_J=0.034\Delta$, SM) in NbTiN or Nb ($\Delta\simeq1.2$--$1.4$\,meV) gives $E_J/h\simeq10$\,GHz,
$I_c\simeq20$\,nA, a $\simeq1$\,GHz mode ($50$\,mK) at $E_J/\hbar\omega=10$, $L\simeq17$\,nH and
$Z\simeq160\,\Omega$ at $\beta=1$: a $\pi$-rf-SQUID at zero applied flux, for which the full DMRG gives a
condensate of $9.6$ photons and $0.94I_c$ (SM). A nA dot junction with a GHz superinductor has
$E_J/\hbar\omega\lesssim3$ and no current-carrying phase for $g\le0.4$ (the $U=8\Delta$ line of
Fig.~\ref{fig:phasemain}): its ground state is the cat state with $\delta\sim\hbar\omega$, visible as a dip of
the $|0\rangle\to|1\rangle$ frequency and a jump of $\langle0|\Phi|1\rangle$ in two-tone spectroscopy, where mean
field predicts a current of $0.6I_c$. SFS junctions ($E_J/\hbar\omega\sim10^2$--$10^4$) are the classical
limit~\cite{Ryazanov2001,Bauer2004}.

\paragraph*{Conclusion.}
Treating the electrons of a quantum-dot Josephson junction and the quantized flux of its loop on the
same footing, we find that the vacuum of the mode controls the junction: its zero-point flux converts a
$0$ junction into a $\pi$ junction, adiabatically through the curvatures of the singlet and doublet
surfaces and non-adiabatically through the Franck--Condon reduction of the level width. Deep in the
adiabatic regime the loop is exactly a fluxonium with the microscopic $E(\varphi)$, the ``photon
condensate'' is the screening instability of a $\pi$ loop, and the exact ground state is the cat state of
the two current directions; the product-state mean field fails at small $E_J/\hbar\omega$ and at the
$0$--$\pi$ boundary. The method extends to Andreev spin qubits with spin--orbit coupling, to multi-dot
junctions, and to a mode coupled to a transmission line.
